The paper has three main claims:

1. **Construction:** gauging transversal Clifford gates can turn Abelian qLDPC codes into non-Abelian ones.
2. **Physics:** these codes retain an analogue of non-Abelian topological phenomena even without an ordinary 2D geometry.
3. **Computation:** gauging and then ungauging measures a logical Clifford gate; this can prepare magic states.

For a first serious reading, I would follow this route:

| Part | Priority | What to take from it |
|---|---:|---|
| I. Preliminaries | Done | The 2D \(D(D_4)\) example is the physical template for everything later. |
| II. Gauging construction | Highest | The conceptual core: why two approaches, and how an ancilla complex makes a logical gate gaugeable. |
| II toy model | High | Read for intuition: same toric-code input, but homological versus graph gauging use different ancilla geometry and give different kinds of physics. |
| Homological formalism | Medium-high | Learn only the dictionary “checks ↔ chain complex” and \(\partial^2=0\); do not get stuck on every chain/cochain convention initially. |
| Two approaches to gauging | Highest | The main fork: **homological gauging** is more structured and can gauge families of gates; **graph gauging** is more general and can gauge one transversal gate, including SWAP. |
| Poincaré duality / cup products | Medium | Important if you want the precise \(D_4\) constructions from CZ gates. On first pass, retain only: duality supplies the required CZ gates and ties them to stabilizer redundancies. |
| Graph gauging | High | Probably the more accessible and broadly applicable construction. Focus on the graph requirements and the SWAP example. |
| Measurement preparation | Medium | Conceptually already familiar from II.C: decoder for the Abelian ancilla code corrects the Abelian measurement byproducts in the non-Abelian code too. |
| III. Worked examples | Selective | Read the opening of each example; choose one to study deeply later. |
| IV. Properties | Highest | Read this after II. Its “single gauge charge” result is the qLDPC analogue of the torus diagnostic you already understand. |
| V. Computation | Highest if you care about fault-tolerant QC | This is arguably the most practically consequential result: gauging + ungauging implements a logical Clifford measurement, which can yield a non-Clifford resource state. |
| Appendices | Leave for later | Use them only when a main-text derivation becomes important to you. |

My suggested reading order is:

\[
\text{II toy model}
\rightarrow
\text{II: two approaches}
\rightarrow
\text{II graph gauging}
\rightarrow
\text{IV properties}
\rightarrow
\text{V computation}
\rightarrow
\text{homological/CZ details and selected examples}.
\]

What you can safely defer on a first pass:

- long explicit cup-product manipulations;
- the detailed ALP fracton construction;
- the addressable-gate example;
- the bivariate bicycle-code formulas;
- appendices deriving individual stabilizers;
- the \(S_3\) fracton appendix.

The most important conceptual distinction to hold onto is:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{homological gauging} & \text{graph gauging}\\
\hline
\text{needs extra code structure (Poincare-like duality)}
&\text{works for a general code with a chosen transversal gate}\\
\text{can gauge a family of related gates}
&\text{typically gauges one global gate}\\
\text{natural for CZ and type-III }D_4\text{-like constructions}
&\text{natural for universal applicability, including SWAP}
\end{array}
\]

In terms of importance: the paper is ambitious and potentially significant because it connects three fields that are usually treated separately—qLDPC code constructions, gauging/topological order, and logical gate measurement. Its strongest broad claim is not merely “here is another non-Abelian code,” but “a transversal Clifford symmetry can be converted into a non-Abelian code construction and then back into a useful logical measurement protocol.”
