# A two-location accepted-output theorem for the repaired tree4 factory

**Claim.** For `RepairedTree` with the screened source and `noisy.OPTIONS` in this directory, every accepted trajectory with at most two faulty elementary locations in the **whole attempt** decodes to the encoded CZ magic state. Immediately before ideal decoding the data lie in the linear span of physical weight-at-most-two Paulis on that state, including data entangled with an environment. The ideal success probability is 3/4. The clean circuit is the same 616-CX, 120-T/T† circuit as the original. The original `ScreenTree` does not satisfy the claim.

This is a compositional proof for a fixed circuit and controller. It uses the existing signed-code, source-history, compact Pauli instrument and terminal recovery lemmas, plus the coordinate and recovery arguments below. The accompanying finite checks use integer arithmetic for support, syndrome and class membership. They do not enumerate full noisy quantum trajectories as a substitute for the proof.

**Model.** Reliable classical computation and storage. Each preparation, reset, gate, destructive measurement/report, and one-qubit waiting or boundary idle can undergo arbitrary action on its participants and an environment. Faults at different locations may be correlated. A faulty readout can report the wrong bit. Rebuilding a source or resource does not reset the fault count. The theorem makes no assertion about leakage, unmodelled couplings, classical controller corruption, or a noise threshold.

## 1. What must be preserved

Write U for the product of the twenty signed local W involutions, and U_v for its four-site factor on block v. On the code U is logical CZ. Put L = span{|++⟩, U|++⟩} in encoded coordinates. U preserves L. Its positive projector maps L onto the one-dimensional line M generated by (|00⟩+|01⟩+|10⟩)/√3. Both vectors in L satisfy every original code stabilizer.

Use diagnostic coordinates only for the argument: conjugate the gauged representation by V, the product of edge-controlled U_v, one for each leaf. V is an involution. Define B_k(L) as the span of E|ψ⟩ tensored with arbitrary graph/environment vectors, for |ψ⟩ in L and E a Pauli supported on at most k inner blocks. The blocks and coefficients may differ between terms. B_k(M) is defined the same way. We show that each new fault increases the allowed block count by at most one on accepted branches, with flag-producing gadgets handled together with their recovery. An honest positive vote replaces L by M. All subsequent closure statements apply also with M in place of L. This last point matters when later votes and reports are faulty.

Expand local fault operators on their actual participants in a Pauli basis with operator-valued environment coefficients. Condition on the **reported** history. Measurement faults on a destroyed wire are general conditional bras and admit the same expansion before an ideal reported-outcome projection. All statements about sums below are linear. A common physical support for every term of a two-fault history is unnecessary for the terminal theorem, which corrects the resulting linear span.

Three identical complete rows include a clean occurrence. The twenty positive/negative generator eigenvalues specify a rank-one source sector; the signed right inverse restores the desired source. Faults after the clean occurrence in accepted source meters leave at most one block per fault modulo a source generator (and at most two for a cancelling pair). ## 3. Routine instruments, including dirty rounds

Every physical data Z check has an even number of sites on every leaf. Its transform by V is the bare data stabilizer. For a bare inner X_B on a leaf the transform is X_B times the edge-controlled Z_B. Indeed U_B X_B U_B = X_B Z_B for these actual signs. On the root there is no edge control. On E applied to an encoded vector, X_B and Z_B each have a definite sign fixed by E. Consequently even an ideal inner-X measurement in a **nonzero** Z sector changes graph coefficients but cannot create new data support or a logical operation. On the positive Z sector it reads the bare inner X. An incorrect Z report therefore cannot invalidate support closure by sending a dirty state down the nominal clean path.

For Pauli meters, push physical fault terms through the remaining Clifford gates to get a data Pauli tail, possible parity/flag flips and an ideal projector. Every zero-flag block-Z outer meter adds at most one block per fault modulo the measured generator, with equal-flag pairs explicitly checked. Every compact inner meter acts within its block. Conjugating any single-block tail by V can involve the associated edge but stays within that data block. A conditional dual-flag meter is used only with at most one remaining fault; on zero flag it adds at most one physical site modulo its measured generator. Multiplication by that generator is harmless on its projected branch. Spectator data or edge idles charge their own single block.

In diagnostic coordinates a leaf Gauss measurement acts as X on its edge. Its entire possibly faulty physical circuit, including the helper, is in one leaf/edge component. Expanding a faulty instrument in that component adds at most one block. The diagnostic root observable is Q = U times the product of the four edge Xs. Its ideal projector preserves B_k(L) and B_k(M): U E = (U E U) U, and the transversal U conjugates E within the same sites. The root flag rejects on any nonzero report. On zero flags, the compiled eight-port skeleton bounds a dirty Pauli tail to at most one port per fault, modulo the full measured product; pairs with cancelling flags are included. For the signed W ports, move the one-site basis rotations to either side of the Clifford meter. Each data wire has one CX port, so a fault on an adjacent rotation transforms into an arbitrary local fault on that site or on its one CX pair. Faults in intervening idles do not enlarge the participants beyond that port. Thus the Clifford skeleton bound applies by linearity to the individually faulty T/CX/T† implementation. In diagnostic coordinates a root-data port costs only the root block, a tree port costs at most its own leaf block, and the full product is Q, which has the closure just proved. This establishes the dirty-round bound without propagating stochastic Paulis through T as though it were Clifford. [Finite root, conditional-meter and sector checks](composition.json)

## 4. Recoveries and the new decoder lemma

A nonzero initial Z report or a nonzero flag requires at least one already charged fault: a fault-free factory has positive code checks and identically clear flags, for both logical branches. At most one fault remains after that trigger. Two consecutive identical complete QND rows thus contain a clean occurrence. Measures/corrections below refer to the syndrome *at that occurrence*. Later ideal commuting checks on a Pauli component preserve its sector; any later zero-flag tail is separately charged. Pauli feedback commutes past such a tail up to phase. This justifies using a matching reference even if its final copy was dirty.

**Z-only recovery.** At a clean Z reference, E = X^x Z^z has x on at most two blocks. The pure-X competitor (x,0) is in the unrestricted data table at this Z syndrome with the X-check bits set to zero. When the controller selects an X correction, its difference from x is an X stabilizer. The implemented gauged feedback is V X_corr V; in diagnostic coordinates it cancels x up to that stabilizer, retaining the old Z support. The nonroot controlled phase and edge–data CZ in the implementation give exactly the conjugate of a local X, since W X W = −sY for sign s. A fault in that feedback is confined to its addressed block/edge component. The next five inner-X reports provide the parity frame. The code consumes it exactly once at the root. If the class is ambiguous, the controller uses a full reference or rejects. In a clean full-reference recovery, correcting the data also removes the pending parity, so a separate parity flip is unnecessary.

**Full recovery.** Each physical coordinate pass decomposes into four disjoint edge/block components. A fault inside either pass, represented relative to its ideal prefix or suffix, therefore costs at most one block. Between passes, code meters are ordinary Pauli instruments, and graph stars act only on graph coordinates. Unflagged full-reference corrections are unique over the two-block envelope. On tree4 the optional joint decoder has the empty cycle word; its candidate families include *every* pair of blocks because all five single-block families have cycle word zero. It cannot turn a genuinely ambiguous data class into a unique class. Star feedback is graph-only in these coordinates, and tree4 has no chord correction.

**Flagged full recovery.** Only inner data flags reach this decision. A compact-root or block-Z outer flag rejects, and conditional history flags reject. Let H be the data tail of one flag-producing fault in the physical inner meter. The ideal projector before that tail preserves the previous error term and its support by §3. With the edge bit zero, H has its ordinary physical signature (x,z). With the edge bit one, its signature is (x,z XOR x), up to phase, because conjugating each X by W gives a signed Y. Expanding in edge projectors is valid for an entangled edge. Any one other fault before the clean full reference contributes an arbitrary term on **one** inner block, including a spectator idle, an earlier surviving error, a coordinate-pass fault or a late conditional-meter tail. Graph factors may change which edge component occurs, and both are included. If both faults are internal to the flagged meter, its entire data action is within that single block. The old candidates already include the whole single-block algebra because a flag-only readout flip supplies a zero data tail. The root block has no coordinate conjugation. This exhausts the global budget at a flagged reference.

The repaired rule vetoes an old unique correction if *any* of the added edge-one competitors with that syndrome has a different stabilizer class. Otherwise all possible terms of the certified syndrome have the chosen class, and the correction restores the encoded vector at the reference. Later errors retain their own charge. This applies also to a nested flag after a gauged X correction: the implementation captures the actual flag at the meter, before either handler dispatches. A nonzero flag handled by the outer vote handler spends a slot; the nested path may still count a vote, which is a dirty slot. The proof counts each physical slot just once.

An independent enumeration uses forward physical flag signatures, the entire one-block algebra for two internal faults, and commutators with both logical Xs and both logical Zs. Its set of unsafe formerly unique entries equals the veto set exactly. This removes 120 of 1,984 formerly unique entries at each of X1–X4 and none at the six other inner meters. The veto is inclusion-minimal among subsets of the **existing** unique decisions retained under this overapproximating envelope. Actual circuit reachability may permit more. ## 5. An honest positive vote and the handoff

Every accepting history contains three distinct positive slots. Assign each fault to its executed slot, including every recovery inside it. Faults in entry preparation belong to entry; faults after voting cannot spoil the count. At least one positive slot is fault-free. For clean entry use §2. For a clean ordinary slot, its positive Z sector either comes from the nine honest reports or from honest successful recovery. With zero Z syndrome on at most two blocks the only X masks are products of full-block X stabilizers. The remaining term is Z^z on L. Five honest inner X reports select |z| mod 2. The leaf edge X eigenvalues and the root Q report select the actual U sign on the errored term. Since U Z^z = (−1)^|z| Z^z U, a corrected positive vote projects the *underlying* vector in L onto M. If full correction restored the code, use z=0. The argument is linear in errors and graph coefficients. A clean positive slot leaves B_k(M); §§3–4 preserve that space with the remaining charges. Thus even two wrong later reports cannot undo a good positive certificate by an undetected logical correction.

In diagnostic coordinates the physical pre-readout state has the ideal V still to undo. Conditioning a physical Z-edge measurement on word w and applying the reported byproducts cancels this V when the bits agree. A wrong bit contributes one U_v on its own leaf. For an arbitrary faulty destructive instrument expand its bra in computational words on the faulty edge; each term has the same one-leaf bound. Charges for feedback-gate faults and waiting idles are local. Thus for f faults through handoff the terminal input is a sum of at-most-f-block errors on M.

## 6. Terminal conversion to physical correctability

Apply the ordered terminal-screen lemma to this input, with at most 2−f faults left. The actual compact screen has the same ideal projectors and the compact zero-flag physical-site spread bound. The recomputed Clifford audit checks its whole zero-report path, including the final boundary, for two incoming blocks, one incoming block plus one screen location, and two screen locations. It tests explicit weight-at-most-two representatives and stabilizer membership. For a nonzero screen, the parent terminal reference proof applies in **original data coordinates**. Its physical flag tails are exactly those of the recorded extraction. An accepted flag repair uses a matching clean reference and the physical flag table, followed by a fresh screen. Generic full recovery uses three matching complete records with epoch resets following a correction. A unique correction at a clean occurrence preserves M, and subsequent accepted zero-flag compact checks leave at most one physical site per remaining fault (two for a cancelling pair). A second-screen fallback uses the same shared budget. All of these cases are covered by the original terminal replacement argument with the compact instrument bound; the new veto is never called here. The distance-six code corrects the entire resulting weight-at-most-two linear span. This proves the claim.
